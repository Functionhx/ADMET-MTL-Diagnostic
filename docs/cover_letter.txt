Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, "Evaluation Protocols Determine the Apparent Benefits of Hard-Sharing Multi-Task Learning in ADMET Prediction," for consideration in Journal of Cheminformatics.

Multi-task learning is widely used in ADMET modeling, and several studies report improvements over matched single-task baselines. These comparisons, however, are typically made under a single evaluation protocol. We present a leakage-controlled diagnostic framework that quantifies when and why hard-sharing multi-task learning improves ADMET prediction under different evaluation protocols.

Our study makes three contributions:

1. **A protocol-contrast estimand for MTL evaluation.** We formalize the question "does the multi-task advantage depend on the evaluation protocol?" as the contrast Gamma = Δ_scaffold − Δ_random between the paired MTL–STL log-loss contrasts under two valid globally partitioned protocols. Across eight TDC ADMET endpoints, the benefit is positive everywhere and significantly larger under scaffold-grouped evaluation (Gamma = +0.10; exact sign test p = 0.008; leave-one-endpoint-out range +0.07 to +0.11).

2. **Leakage-controlled evaluation.** Global molecule allocation prevents test molecules from reaching the multi-task model through auxiliary endpoints (cross-task identity exposure), a failure mode we demonstrate can invert conclusions.

3. **A calibration diagnostic with a sharp finding.** A nested, per-run temperature calibration attenuates the raw advantage—and the protocol interaction itself—to approximately zero, showing that apparent multi-task proper-score gains can reflect temperature-correctable differences in raw logit scale rather than ranking information or a durable calibration advantage. The novelty dependence of the benefit also reverses direction across protocols.

We believe the manuscript fits the journal's scope on machine learning in chemistry, molecular property prediction, and reproducibility/benchmarking. The code, split manifests, and prediction tables are released openly at https://github.com/Functionhx/ADMET-MTL-Diagnostic.
